You retain copyright in your contribution. You only grant a license.

## Why a CLA

Yukti is open-source first (Apache 2.0). However, the project needs the right to relicense in the future — for example, to switch to a more restrictive license (BSL, SSPL) if a hyperscaler captures the project's value, to dual-license commercially, or to contribute to a foundation (CNCF, ASF).

Without CLAs from every contributor, that flexibility is **irreversibly lost** the moment the first external pull request lands. CLAs protect the project's options. This is the same pattern used by Apache Software Foundation, Linux Foundation, Google, Microsoft, Pulumi, Grafana Labs, HashiCorp, and most serious open-source projects with commercial ambition.

## How signing works

This CLA will be enforced automatically by a CLA-bot on pull requests (via [cla-assistant.io](https://cla-assistant.io) or equivalent). Your first PR will prompt you to sign electronically. Subsequent PRs from the same GitHub account are recognized automatically.

While CLA-bot integration is being set up (early project stage), contributors should include the following line in their pull-request description:

> *I agree to the Yukti CLA (see CLA.md).*

By signing, you represent that:

2. Your contribution is your original work (or you have the right to contribute work originating from someone else, and you've identified that work in the PR).
3. Your contribution does not knowingly infringe any third party's intellectual property rights.

## DCO compatibility

Yukti also accepts the Developer Certificate of Origin sign-off (`git commit --signoff`) as an additional signal — but DCO alone does **not** grant relicensing rights. The CLA is required for the relicensing flexibility above; DCO is welcomed as a supplementary attestation.
